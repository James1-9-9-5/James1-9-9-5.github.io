About Me

I've always been drawn to the space where geography meets real-world impact. What started as a fascination with maps turned into a career analysing spatial data, building GIS systems, and helping organisations make better decisions about the land, resources, and communities they work with.


Some of the work I'm proudest of sits outside a typical office brief — mapping vegetation for anti-poaching efforts, tracking endangered species across shifting terrain, spending time in Bolivia co-managing volunteers at an animal rescue sanctuary. GIS, for me, has never just been about the software. It's a way of understanding the world a little more clearly, and occasionally doing some good while I'm at it.


Outside of work, I chase the same instinct in a different form — travel, the outdoors, and the occasional questionable decision to jump off a bridge attached to a giant elastic band. If there's a map involved, or a reasonable amount of adrenaline, I'm probably interested.


Where I've Worked

My path into GIS wasn't entirely linear. I spent my early twenties in admissions, market research, and customer-facing roles — work that taught me how to manage people, data, and deadlines long before I ever touched a mapping tool professionally. There's a version of this story where none of that experience mattered once I moved into GIS, but the opposite has been true: knowing how to communicate clearly, manage competing priorities, and actually listen to what people need has shaped how I approach every project since.


The real turning point came through conservation work — internships and fieldwork mapping vegetation, tracking endangered species, and supporting anti-poaching efforts. It was there that GIS stopped being just software to me and became a way of understanding a landscape, a population, a problem, all at once. That internship led to technician work classifying land cover, which led to analyst roles where I started solving genuine operational problems with spatial data — automating reports, cleaning up databases, building tools that people actually used day to day.


Today, I lead the design and deployment of full GIS and asset management systems, working across the entire stack from field data collection to dashboards leadership actually looks at. But the instinct that got me here hasn't changed much from those early days in the field: give me a dataset, a map, and a problem worth solving, and I'm happy.
