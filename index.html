The Long Way Round

I stumbled into GIS, to be honest. I met it as a module in my Geography degree at Brighton and never thought it could be a career. After uni I spent a few years trying different jobs, never quite finding the right fit, until COVID gave me time to rethink things. GIS kept coming back to me, so I packed up and went to South Africa for an internship alongside an Anti-Poaching Unit. It’s no coincidence I picked a game reserve. I’ve always loved the natural world, and getting to use maps for conservation was the perfect way to combine the two.

That love of nature goes back further than South Africa. In 2019 I spent three months at Parque Machía in Bolivia, helping look after the animals and keeping the place running day to day. It’s also where I ended up with a coati on my lap, which is easily one of my favourite photos. Looking back, Bolivia and the reserve in South Africa feel like two chapters of the same story, and they’re a big part of why I care about doing work that makes a real difference on the ground.

Outside of work, you’ll usually find me somewhere outdoors. I love hiking and bouldering, and I hit the gym a few times a week to balance out all the hours spent mapping. Bouldering is my favourite because it’s about solving a problem one move at a time, stumbling a few times, and getting that satisfaction when it finally clicks, which is exactly how I approach my work. Travel is the other thing I can’t get enough of. Spending time in South Africa, Bolivia and now Canada has shown me how much I enjoy being dropped into somewhere new, meeting people from different backgrounds and seeing how they see the world. It’s made me a lot more curious and adaptable, and it’s a big reason I love working with different communities today.

None of it would be the same without family and friends, who keep me grounded. Moving between countries has made me value the time I get with them even more, and the best days are usually the simple ones: good food, good company and a bit of fresh air. Whatever I’m up to, they’re who I want to share it with.

That’s really the thread through everything, whether it’s the rangers in South Africa, the team in Bolivia or my colleagues in London (this is me on my last day with them). The people you work with matter just as much as the work itself. If you’re building real relationships and having a laugh through the daily grind, it makes everything else better, and that’s something I try to bring to everyone I work with now.
